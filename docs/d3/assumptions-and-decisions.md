The registers of D1 and D2 of both parts remain in force and are not repeated. Both parts already use `A-…` and `D-…` identifiers with overlapping numbers, so the items of this deliverable, which concern the whole team, use the prefixes **D3-A** (assumptions) and **D3-D** (decisions).

The first items come from the comparison of the ADC implementation with the ADC ↔ Monitoring interface contract v1.0 (Appendix A), made when the two parts started their integration.

## 6.1 New assumptions

| ID | Assumption | Source | Affects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D3-A-01 | One medication "unit" counted by the ADC is one "box" of the interface contract: the ADC reports its recorded quantities as they are in `quantityInBoxes`, with no conversion. | Team, comparison of the ADC with the contract | Contract §2 and §3.2 (`quantityInBoxes`); ADC 20-unit container limit; Monitoring low-stock threshold (FR-INV-03, below 10 boxes) |
| D3-A-02 | The clock of the ADC is synchronized with the hospital time server: the link converts the local times of the ADC records to UTC as they are, without correcting them. | Team, ADC network link | Contract §2; Part B A-14 |

## 6.2 New decisions

| ID | Decision | Rationale | Affects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D3-D-01 | The patient name does not cross the interface: a dispensing transaction carries a `patientId` (UUID v4) only, and the ADC keeps the name locally. | No requirement of the Monitoring System needs the name, and sending it would put health information on the interface for nothing (PHIPA). Part A FR-COM-14 already limits the transmitted inventory information to what the agreed interface requires. | Part A FR-COM-14; contract §2 and §3.2; Part B A-21, NFR-SEC-04 |
| D3-D-02 | The remote unlock of the ADC prototype is left out of the integration: the only command exchanged is `set_maintenance_mode` (contract §4.1, §5.4). | The demo scenarios do not need it, and letting the Monitoring System unlock a drawer is a safety risk that would need its own hazard analysis before being added to the contract. | Part A FR-COM-05, FR-COM-07; contract §4.1 |
| D3-D-03 | The ADC does not send its low-inventory warning as an alarm. | The Monitoring System already flags low stock from the complete inventory of each heartbeat; an alarm as well would show the same condition twice on the dashboard. | Part A low-inventory fault; Part B FR-INV-03 |
| D3-D-04 | ADC faults are sent as alarms with these types: `DRAWER_UNLOCK` and `DRAWER_CLOSE` → `dispensing_failure`, `SESSION_DOOR_OPEN` → `door_left_open`, any other internal fault → `hardware_fault`; the severity keeps its level, in lowercase. | Only the types of the contract (§5.2) get a meaningful label on the dashboard; the mapping is done once, in the ADC communication module. | Contract §5.2, §5.3; Part A FaultManager |
| D3-D-05 | The Inventory Supervisor announces, after storing a new inventory, the list of medications flagged low stock in that ADC whenever this list or its quantities change; the Operator Dashboard pushes it on a live channel `inventory`, seen by the roles that see the ADC statuses. The Inventory Supervisor now exists once in the running system, like the other modules that keep subscribers (D-37). | During the first run with the real ADC, the "Low stock" box of the dashboard only changed on reload, while statuses and alarms are live (D-22, D-27): a nurse could rely on a list that no longer matches the cabinets. Announcing only real changes avoids one push per heartbeat. | Part B FR-INV-03, NFR-USA-01, D-27, D-36, D-37 |
| D3-D-06 | Only a completed dispense with a confirmed quantity of at least one unit is sent as a `dispensing` transaction; a started, cancelled or zero-quantity withdrawal is not sent. | The ADC rewrites its audit event in place when a withdrawal is confirmed: sending it earlier would report a dispense as started and never as completed, and a transaction sent again must be identical (Part B A-26). | Contract §3.2; Part B A-26, FR-COM-09; Part A FR-DISP-06 |
| D3-D-07 | Each ADC identifier that crosses the interface (cabinet, medication, patient, staff member, transaction) gets a random UUID v4 the first time it is sent, kept with the `adcId` in a file of the ADC, so it never changes, even after a restart. | The contract requires UUID v4 identifiers, while the ADC uses its own (`MED-001`, usernames, `EVT-…`); a mapping in the link avoids changing every ADC module. | Contract §2, §3.1; Part B A-21, A-22 |
| D3-D-08 | A command received from the Monitoring System is applied on the ADC under a system account, `monitoring-system`, with Level 3 rights; it is recorded in the ADC audit log under that name and cannot be used to log in. | Entering maintenance requires a Level 3 user on the ADC, and nobody may be logged in when the command arrives; a named account keeps the change attributable. | Contract §4.1; Part A FR-MODE-03, FR-LOG-07 |
| D3-D-09 | The ADC refuses `set_maintenance_mode` with `409 COMMAND_NOT_APPLICABLE` while it is in fault, or while a drawer is open or a dispense awaits confirmation. | Switching mode in the middle of a withdrawal would quarantine the medication being taken; in fault, maintenance is not allowed by the ADC rules. | Contract §4.1, §6.2; Part A INV-DISP-3 |
| D3-D-10 | The `screenLockTimeoutSeconds` setting (15 to 600 s) sets the length of the ADC lock countdown, so the screen locks after 15 s without input plus that countdown; `timeServer` is stored by the ADC but not used, the prototype having no clock synchronization. | Part A FR-AUTH-04 fixes the 15 s before the countdown and lets only the countdown (1 to 900 s) be configured; the contract range fits inside it. | Contract §4.2; Part A FR-AUTH-04 |
| D3-D-11 | Failed logins lock only the account that failed, for the configured duration, after which it can log in again; a Level 2/3 recovery login clears every lockout. | One counter shared by all accounts let three mistakes of one user lock out the whole ward (F-ADC-05). | SR-ADC-05; Part A FR-AUTH-06, INV-AUTH-4 |
| D3-D-12 | When the ADC audit log reaches 90 % of its capacity, the ADC raises a Warning `AUDIT_LOG_NEARLY_FULL`, shown on the cabinet and sent to the Monitoring System as a `hardware_fault` alarm. | A full log blocks every protected operation, including login, with no warning beforehand (F-ADC-06). | SR-ADC-05; D3-D-04; Part A FR-LOG-09, FR-LOG-12 |
