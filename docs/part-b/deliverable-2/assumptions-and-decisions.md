# Assumptions & Decisions since Deliverable 1 - Part B (Hospital Monitoring System)

This section lists every assumption and decision made since Deliverable 1, with the reasoning behind each one. The D1 register (A-01 to A-17, D-01 to D-19, the role permissions matrix of D-14 and the D1 ethical and accessibility considerations) is still in force and is not repeated; numbering continues from it.

The new items come from three sources:

- **the interface contract agreed with the ADC team (Part A)**, which closed the four open points of D1 (A-18 to A-24, see the introduction);
- **the precise specification**, whose complete tables exposed cases that D1 left open (A-25, D-20 to D-26, D-40; the gaps are listed in the precise specification, section 4);
- **the modular design and the implementation** (A-26, D-27 to D-39, D-41 to D-44), each decision stating which module it concerns and which anticipated change it protects.

## 1. Changes to D1 items

| ID | Change | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A-08 | An ADC also sends a heartbeat at once when it changes its operational state, not only when it raises an alarm. | Agreed with Part A; a state change (e.g. FAULT) must reach the dashboard within the 2 s of D-22. |
| A-13 | A dispensing activity also carries the medication name and the staff member's name. | Reports must show readable names (A-21: identifiers are UUIDs). |

## 2. New assumptions

| ID | Assumption | Source | Affects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A-18 | The ADCs and the Monitoring System exchange JSON messages over HTTPS. Both expose an HTTP server: the Monitoring System receives registrations and heartbeats; each ADC receives commands and configurations at the contact address it gave when registering. The full contract is in the ADC ↔ Monitoring interface contract (interface-adc-monitoring.md). | Team, with ADC team (Part A) | FR-COM-01, FR-COM-04, FR-COM-05, FR-COM-07, NFR-SEC-05 |
| A-19 | Every acknowledgement is the synchronous response to the request it acknowledges: the response to a heartbeat gives a status ("received", "rejected") per transaction; the response of an ADC to a command or a configuration repeats its identifier and the ADC identifier. | Team, with ADC team (Part A) | FR-REQ-01, FR-COM-06, FR-COM-11, D-07 |
| A-20 | An ADC puts its identifier in every message it sends; the ADC identifier of a heartbeat is the originating ADC of every transaction it contains. | Team, with ADC team (Part A) | FR-COM-09, FR-ALM-02, NFR-SEC-03 |
| A-21 | Every identifier exchanged between both systems (ADC, medication, patient, staff member, transaction, command, configuration) is a UUID v4; an ADC generates its identifier once and keeps it across restarts. ADCs, medications and staff members also have a label (ADC label, medication name, staff member name) used only for display; patients have none, to keep health information off the interface. | Team, with ADC team (Part A) | FR-COM-01, FR-COM-02, FR-COM-09, FR-INV-01, FR-INV-06, FR-DSH-03, NFR-SEC-04 |
| A-22 | An ADC registers at every start-up, always with the same identifier, and registers again whenever the Monitoring System answers that it is not registered. | Team, with ADC team (Part A) | FR-COM-01, FR-COM-02 |
| A-23 | When the Monitoring System does not answer, or answers with an error, the ADC keeps its transactions and tries again at the heartbeat rhythm (30 s); it sends at most one heartbeat at a time. | Team, with ADC team (Part A) | A-06, NFR-REL-01 |
| A-24 | The memory of an ADC is large enough to keep every unacknowledged transaction during any outage. | Team, with ADC team (Part A) | A-06, NFR-REL-01 |
| A-25 | Each operator workstation has a working speaker, switched on and audible from where the users work. | Team (D2 precise specification, section 4) | NFR-ACC-01, NFR-SAF-04 |
| A-26 | A transaction sent again by an ADC (after a lost response or an outage) is identical to the first sending: same identifier, same content. A transaction already recorded therefore passes its checks again, so checking it before looking for its identifier gives the acknowledgement of SPEC-COM-03. | Team (D2 implementation of SPEC-COM-03) | FR-COM-08, D-07, D-28 |

## 3. New decisions

| ID | Decision | Rationale | Affects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D-20 | A registration whose ADC identifier is already registered is accepted: it replaces the label, the contact address and the location of that ADC and is logged, without changing its connectivity status, its reported state or its alarms. | An ADC that restarts must rejoin without an administrator. The risk of a device taking over the identifier of an ADC is limited by the encrypted channel (NFR-SEC-05) and by the logging of every re-registration. | FR-COM-02, FR-HIS-08 |
| D-21 | Between its first registration and its first heartbeat, an ADC is displayed with the status PENDING. | It has no reported state yet, and none of the other labels is true: showing ONLINE would claim a health never reported, UNREACHABLE an outage that did not happen. | FR-HLT-01, FR-HLT-06, NFR-SAF-03, D-16 |
| D-22 | A change of the displayed status of an ADC (reported state or connectivity) reaches the dashboard within 2 s, the bound already set for alarms. | A cabinet going OFFLINE matters as much as an alarm; one bound for both is simpler to verify. | FR-DSH-01, FR-HLT-01 to 05, NFR-PRF-01 |
| D-23 | Only ADCs whose last heartbeat is less than 45 s old are proposed as same-floor sources of a medication in low stock. | The stock of an unreachable or offline ADC is stale: proposing it could send a nurse to an empty or broken cabinet. | FR-INV-04, FR-INV-05, NFR-SAF-01 |
| D-24 | A medication missing from the inventory of an ADC is not flagged "Low Stock" for that ADC. | The system has no list of what each ADC should hold; it can only judge the quantities an ADC reports. | FR-INV-03 |
| D-25 | When an account is deactivated, its open sessions are closed: the next request of that user leads to the login page, where the account is refused. | A deactivated user (e.g. who left the hospital) must lose access at once, not at the end of a session. | FR-USR-05, NFR-SEC-01 |
| D-26 | A request refused because of the user's role (acknowledgement, command, configuration) is not written to the audit log; only the permission message is shown. | The audit log records actions that were performed (NFR-AUD-01); the role check itself prevents the action. | NFR-AUD-01, NFR-SEC-02 |
| D-27 | Only the Operator Dashboard module knows how information reaches the browser (Server-Sent Events through Transmit). The Alarm Manager and the Cabinet Health Monitor announce their changes to subscribers; the Dashboard turns them into live events and sounds. | The delivery technology to the workstation (SSE today, WebSockets or a native client tomorrow) is a likely change; it must touch the Dashboard only. | NFR-PRF-01, NFR-SAF-04, NFR-ACC-01, D-22 |
| D-28 | A transaction already received is detected by the module that records it: recording an alarm or a dispensing activity answers "recorded" or "duplicate", and the Historical Data Store guarantees it by keeping each transaction identifier at most once, alarms and dispensing activities together. | The ADC Communication Manager only translates this answer into an acknowledgement; it keeps no list of identifiers, so the duplicate rule cannot drift away from what is actually stored. | FR-COM-08, FR-REQ-01, D-07 |
| D-29 | Every module reads the current time from a single Clock module, injected into it; tests replace it by a clock they control. | The 45 s, 75 s and 10 s rules and the retention periods can be tested at their exact boundaries without waiting for real time. A demonstration that must jump forward in time only needs another Clock (AC-01). | FR-HLT-02, FR-HLT-03, FR-COM-06, FR-HIS-09 |
| D-30 | A refusal that the specification foresees (alarm already acknowledged, ADC offline, command failed) is a result of the operation; a violated pre-condition or an internal failure (unknown ADC or alarm, storage unavailable) is an exception. | The caller must handle every foreseen refusal, because the type of the result lists it; exceptions are kept for what should never happen in normal use. | FR-ALM-06, FR-COM-06, FR-COM-12 |
| D-31 | Role checks are made once, on the server, by the Operator Dashboard controllers asking User Management before they call another module; the browser never decides a permission. The other modules state the permission as a pre-condition and do not check it again. | The role → permission matrix (D-14) is in a single module, so changing it touches User Management only. | FR-USR-02, NFR-SEC-02, D-14 |
| D-32 | The contact address of an ADC is kept by the ADC Communication Manager, not by the Cabinet Health Monitor. | An address only makes sense for the transport in use; changing the transport must not touch the module that tracks ADC health. | FR-COM-01, FR-COM-02, FR-COM-04 |
| D-33 | The event history and the audit log are read through the Reporting module, not directly from the Historical Data Store as drawn in the D1 architecture. | Reporting is the only module that filters patient identifiers by role and logs their display; the Historical Data Store returns everything it holds. | FR-HIS-04, FR-HIS-07, NFR-SEC-04, NFR-AUD-06 |
| D-34 | Times are stored to the second: the milliseconds of the timestamps set by an ADC are dropped when stored. | The 45 s, 75 s and 10 s rules, the retention periods and the display of events all work at the scale of the second; the standard date columns of the storage layer keep seconds only. | A-14, NFR-REL-01 |
| D-35 | Outside production, an ADC may register an `http://` contact address; in production only `https://` is accepted (NFR-SEC-05). | The simulated ADCs used for tests and the demonstration run on the presenter's machine without TLS certificates. | NFR-SEC-05, FR-COM-01 |
| D-36 | A module that announces a change calls each listener only after the change is stored; a listener that fails is logged and neither stops the other listeners nor undoes the change. | A display problem on one dashboard must never lose an alarm: if the failure reached the ADC Communication Manager, the ADC would resend the alarm, which would then be a duplicate and never be announced. | NFR-SAF-04, NFR-PRF-01, D-27, D-28 |
| D-37 | The modules that keep subscribers in memory (Alarm Manager, Cabinet Health Monitor) exist once in the running system; every other module may be created on each use. | The Dashboard must subscribe to the same Alarm Manager that the ADC Communication Manager calls, otherwise it never hears about new alarms. | D-27, FR-ALM-04, NFR-PRF-01 |
| D-38 | When an ADC goes offline, its communication loss alarm is stored before its connectivity becomes offline; if the storage fails in between, the next check tries again. | A second alarm for the same loss is acceptable; an offline ADC without an alarm is not. | FR-HLT-04, NFR-SAF-04, D-15 |
| D-39 | Records are kept in a SQLite database (a single file, no database server), through the default data layer of the framework (Lucid). | Alarms and audit entries must survive a restart and be kept for one year (FR-HIS-01, NFR-REL-01), so state in memory is not enough; SQLite is the lightest durable storage, with nothing to install or run beside the application (best practices: avoid unnecessary complexity). The choice is the secret of the Historical Data Store (AC-09): moving to another engine touches that module only. | FR-HIS-01 to 09, NFR-REL-01, NFR-AUD-02 |
| D-40 | The first registration of an ADC records its first status change (no reported state, reachable), as for any later change. | The history of an ADC (FR-HIS-04) then starts when the ADC appears, and an ADC that never sends a heartbeat still has a recorded status before it goes unreachable. | FR-HIS-03, FR-HIS-04, FR-HLT-06 |
| D-41 | Reports name each ADC by its label, read from the Cabinet Health Monitor at generation time; Reporting therefore uses the Cabinet Health Monitor. | FR-RPT-06 asks for the ADC of each alarm, and an identifier is unreadable for users; the label belongs to the registry of ADCs (D-32 keeps the contact address apart, but not the label). The USES relation stays without cycle. | FR-RPT-06, NFR-USA-02 |
| D-42 | A report is saved with all its figures, patient identifiers included, and filtered for each viewer when it is shown; the list of saved reports shows a role only the kinds of reports it may generate. | A pharmacist and an administrator who reopen the same report see what they are allowed to see, each display being audited (NFR-AUD-06); risk management never sees a consumption report in its list (US-RPT-07). | FR-RPT-08, NFR-SEC-04, D-11, D-14 |
| D-43 | Times are stored in UTC and always shown in the time zone of the hospital (setting `DISPLAY_TIME_ZONE`, America/Toronto by default), by the server pages and by the live dashboard alike; the days of a report or history period are days of that time zone. | A time read on the dashboard and the same time read in a report or the event history must be equal, whatever the time zone of the server or of the workstation. | FR-HIS-04, FR-RPT-01, FR-RPT-06, NFR-USA-02 |
| D-44 | The Monitoring System is a single TypeScript application on the AdonisJS framework: one server serves both the dashboard pages (rendered on the server) and the ADC interface (`/api/v1`). | One process and one port to run on the demonstration machine (best practices: avoid unnecessary complexity). The framework's dependency-injection container is what wires the modules through their interfaces and lets a test replace one (D-29); TypeScript types make the result types of D-30 checked by the compiler; pages rendered on the server keep every permission decision there (D-31). | All modules; NFR-SEC-02 |

## 4. Ethical and accessibility considerations in the design

The D1 principles are unchanged. The table below says how the design and the code now enforce them.

| Principle | How the design enforces it | Decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Patient safety | The connectivity detected by the system overrides what an ADC reports, and is checked every second (`checkTimeouts`). The communication loss alarm is stored before the ADC is shown offline, so an offline cabinet never goes unnoticed. A display failure on one dashboard can never lose an alarm. A command is never assumed delivered without the ADC's acknowledgement. | D-04, D-23, D-36, D-38 |
| Privacy (PHIPA) | Patient identifiers never cross the ADC interface with a name (A-21). The rule that hides them from some roles lives in one module (Reporting), which every history and report view goes through; each display is audited. | D-33, D-42 |
| Controlled access | Every page needs an authenticated, active account; a deactivated account loses its open sessions. Roles are checked once, on the server, by asking User Management, which holds the only copy of the permission matrix; the live channels are authorized the same way. | D-25, D-31 |
| Accountability | The Historical Data Store exports no operation to modify or delete a record (other than the retention purge); an acknowledgement is stored apart from its alarm so that the alarm is never rewritten. A command's sending and its outcome are two audit entries, so a sending without outcome stays visible. Times are shown in the hospital time zone everywhere. | D-28, D-39, D-43 |
| Accessibility (AODA) | Alarms are announced in three ways: a page flash, a sound and a text read by screen readers (`aria-live`). Colour is only added to text labels that are always shown. The workstation must have a working speaker. | A-25, D-16, D-27 |
| Honesty about limits | A cabinet between registration and first heartbeat is shown PENDING, not ONLINE. Only cabinets heard from in the last 45 s are proposed as stock sources. A missing medication is not reported as low stock, because the system cannot know it should be there. | D-21, D-23, D-24 |
